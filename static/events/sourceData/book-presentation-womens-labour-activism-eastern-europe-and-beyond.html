Book presentation: Women’s Labour Activism in Eastern Europe and Beyond 

06 October 2026 - 16:00

Women’s Labour Activism in Eastern Europe and Beyond presents an inclusive history of women’s labour activism in Eastern Europe and beyond from the mid-nineteenth to the late twentieth century. This collaborative monograph explores women's activism and organising to improve the working conditions and living circumstances of lower-income and working-class women and their communities in the region and internationally. 

On 6 October, Susan Zimmermann and Ivelina Masheva, two of the book's ten authors, will discuss its key themes. 

By unravelling, reconsidering and connecting women’s labour activism over a long period of time and under diverse political regimes, the book reveals an impressive level of persistence. The activists responded not only to new challenges, but also to fundamental, unresolved issues such as unpaid work. Because the world of work remained enormously gendered, and male colleagues took little or no interest in improving women’s working conditions, labour activists needed to continue addressing it, even through forms of everyday activism.

As a team of ten authors, they needed to work with coherent terminology from the beginning. Consequently, Women’s Labour Activism opens with a vocabulary of concepts and terminology that establishes the conceptual framework for an integrative, global history of labour activism. This is followed by ten region-wide chapters, each organised around a specific theme. These chapters are connected by a collectively developed conceptual approach. Each of the ten chapters transcends historical and political ruptures, as well as political divides between women labour activists and their organisations, embodying their move beyond inherited historiographic and social movement compartmentalisations.

Susan Zimmermann (Central European University, Austria) is a historian of labour, gender, and internationalism in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. Recently, she has written on interwar transnational debates and initiatives connecting minimum wages, equal pay, and women’s unpaid work. She is currently working on a book manuscript, co-authored with Dorothy Sue Cobble, on the transnational political biography of Tony (Toni) Sender during the decades she spent in the United States and at the United Nations. 

Ivelina Masheva is an assistant professor at the Institute for Historical Studies, Bulgarian Academy of Sciences. She holds a PhD in History from the University of Sofia (2015). Between 2020 and 2026, she participated in the ERC-funded project ZARAH: Women’s Labour Activism in Eastern Europe and Transnationally, from the Age of Empires to the Late 20th Century. Her research interests include women’s and gender history, labour history, and legal history.
